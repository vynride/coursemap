Problem statement

Students learn from YouTube. Exams come from the syllabus.

Most of us prepare for university exams from free YouTube lecture playlists. A playlist is made for everyone; the exam is set from our own university's syllabus, in its own question pattern.

  • No idea what's missing. Nobody tells you which syllabus topics the playlist skips. You find out in the exam hall.
  • Topics are buried in hours of video. Finding where "Banker's algorithm" is explained means scrubbing through 12 lectures.
  • No practice in the real format. Watching isn't answering a 7-mark question the way your examiner marks it.

Who has this problem: every student who learns from a playlist instead of paid coaching. That's most of our classmates.

Proposed solution

3

Syllabus map

Every topic marked Taught Mentioned Missing. One click jumps the player to the exact minute. Missing topics get suggested videos.

4

Exam practice

Questions per unit in your exam's pattern. Write an answer, get marked like an examiner, then "rewatch where this is taught".

For the student

Knows exactly what to study beyond the playlist, finds any topic in one click, practises in the real format.

Grounded, not generated

Every map entry and question points to a real moment in a real lecture. Gemini can't invent timestamps (see guardrails).

Built once, used by the class

Analyses are cached per video and syllabus, so the second student with the same playlist gets results instantly.

Every Google service does essential work

{{icon:googlegemini}}
Gemini on Vertex AI
The core of the product

Video understanding on YouTube URLs. Gemini watches a public lecture straight from its link: no downloading, no separate transcription, no vector database.

Cost-tuned for lectures. media_resolution=LOW and a low frame rate, since audio carries most of a lecture.

Native PDF reading for the syllabus, including tables and scanned pages.

Structured output (response_schema): every call returns typed JSON we validate, not free text.

Four jobs: parse syllabus · map lectures · set exam questions · grade answers.

{{icon:youtube}}
YouTube Data API v3
Playlist + discovery

playlistItems.list and videos.list give the lecture list, durations and privacy (to skip private and live videos). search.list finds videos for missing topics.

{{icon:cloudrun}}
Cloud Run
Serving + background jobs

One container serves the React app and the API. A warm instance with CPU always allocated runs the multi-minute analysis job after the request returns.

{{icon:firebase}}
Firestore
State + shared cache

Job progress the browser polls, and an analysis cache keyed by video + syllabus: a class of 60 on one playlist pays for one analysis.

{{icon:secretmanager}}
Secret Manager + IAM
No keys in code

YouTube key lives in Secret Manager. Vertex AI and Firestore use the Cloud Run service account, so there are no Gemini keys at all.

{{icon:cloudbuild}}

Technical depth

We don't trust the model blindly

🎯

Topics come from a closed list

Gemini gets the syllabus as numbered topic IDs (U3.T2) and may only answer with those. Unknown IDs are dropped.

⏱️

Timestamps are checked

Every timestamp is parsed and checked against the video's real duration from the YouTube API. Invalid ones fall back to the video start.

🔗

Questions can't invent sources

We hand Gemini a numbered list of moments we extracted. A question cites a moment by number or says "not in your playlist".

🧩

Partial failure is fine

If one lecture fails to analyse, the job continues and lists it as skipped. Private, deleted and live videos are skipped up front.

📐

Typed JSON everywhere

Structured output plus Pydantic validation on every response. Scores are clamped to the question's marks.

🧪

Tested where it matters

Unit tests cover parsing, validation, coverage and the full pipeline with a fake model and a fake YouTube API.

Scope

What we built today, and what comes next

In scope (built in this hackathon)

  • Public YouTube playlists up to 12 lectures, or a single video
  • Syllabus as PDF upload or pasted text
  • Exam pattern as free text, with presets
  • Syllabus map: Taught / Mentioned / Missing, timestamp jumps
  • Suggested videos for missing topics
  • Exam-pattern questions per unit + examiner-style grading
  • Deployed on Cloud Run, cached in Firestore

Out of scope (roadmap)

  • Hindi and regional-language answers and feedback
  • Previous-year papers to weight topics by how often they're asked
  • Class view for teachers: which topics the class is weakest on
  • Spaced-repetition revision plan to the exam date
  • Accounts, saved progress, Android app
  • Creator view: what a playlist lacks for each university
Feasibility & viability

Small to build, cheap to run

Why it fits in 6 hours

  • Gemini reads YouTube links and PDFs natively, so there's no download, transcription or embedding pipeline to build.
  • Structured output gives typed JSON, so no fragile text parsing.
  • One container, one deploy command.

Running cost

At low media resolution (~66 tokens per frame) and 0.2 frames/sec plus audio (~32 tokens/sec), one lecture-hour is roughly (13 + 32) × 3,600 ≈ 160k input tokens.

Cached per video + syllabus, so every later student on the same playlist costs close to zero.

Impact & benefits

Exam alignment for students who can't pay for coaching

Students

  • See syllabus gaps weeks before the exam, not in the hall
  • Any topic in one click instead of scrubbing hours of video
  • Practise in the real pattern, with feedback

Colleges & teachers

  • Point students to vetted playlists, with gaps flagged
  • Class-level view of weak topics (roadmap)
  • Works with the syllabus they already publish

Creators

  • Views still go to their videos through the embed
  • Learn which syllabus topics their playlist is missing

Social impact

Coaching gives paid students a syllabus-aligned course and test series. CourseMap gives the same alignment to anyone with a free playlist and a phone, at a cost that falls as more classmates use it.

How we'd measure it

  • Syllabus coverage found per playlist
  • Practice score on first vs. second attempt
  • Share of students who study a flagged gap topic
